## What it pays for

quello is MIT and has one maintainer. The interesting half — picking an element, resolving it to a
component — was written once. The half that never ends is the other one: eleven playgrounds that
have to keep agreeing with the [compatibility matrix](/reference/compatibility) while Vite, webpack,
Next, Nuxt, Astro and the rest move underneath them.

That upkeep is invisible when it works, and it is the first thing to stop when there is no time for
it. Sponsorship buys the time.

## What each tier carries

Amounts are written down in exactly one place — the
[sponsors page](https://github.com/sponsors/Linko91) — so they can change without a release here.

| Tier | Where you appear |
| --- | --- |
| **Supporter** | Your name on this page and in [`SPONSORS.md`](https://github.com/Linko91/quello/blob/main/SPONSORS.md). |
| **Backer** | Your name and a link, in both, and in the README. |
| **Partner** | Your logo and a link in all of the above, plus the docs homepage. |

A one-time sponsorship carries the same thanks and no listing: it is a tip, not a placement.

::note
Sponsoring buys no priority and no private support — the issue tracker stays first-come. It buys the
time to keep that matrix honest.
::

## Other ways to help

Sponsorship is not the only currency, and at `0.1.x` it is not the most useful one:

- **Report what broke.** A pick that resolves to the wrong component, on a framework version nobody
  has tried, is worth more than a month of sponsorship.
- **Add a row to the matrix.** Rails, Laravel, Ember, Qwik — the [CLI route](/reference/cli) should
  already work, and a confirmation from a real project turns a `○` into a `✅`.
- **Tell someone.** A line in an awesome-list, or a mention where your team argues about tooling,
  reaches people no launch day does.
